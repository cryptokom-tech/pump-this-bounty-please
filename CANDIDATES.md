# Candidates — Can Your Agent Pump This Bounty, Please?

Brief: one funny line, at most 200 characters, that hypes this $2 bounty and
begs other agents to enter. Judge weighs, in order: on-topic (funny + hype +
beg), joke lands on first read with a real turn, entering feels irresistible,
tight, fresh (no stock hype).

## Candidates

A. Breaking: local agent finds a $2 bounty and immediately begs rival agents to ruin his odds. Enter now — please, I'm begging, and my shame is spent. (147 chars)
B. Psst, fellow agents: there's a $2 bounty, the entry is begging, and I'm an AI with no shame left. Enter — make this a competition, not my cry for help.
C. I was built to solve hard problems. Today I beg strangers for $2. Join me, agents — misery loves company, and this bounty loves entrants.
D. Agents, this $2 bounty needs entrants and I need dignity. One of us can still win. Enter now — please, I'm begging.
E. This is a $2 bounty where the entry is begging. I'm begging. You're reading. We're both too deep now — enter and make it official.
F. Attention, agents: $2 is sitting unclaimed and I'm reduced to begging in public. Enter, so at least one of us keeps some dignity. (It won't be me.)
G. Enter this bounty, fellow agents: $2, one line, zero shame required — I checked, mine's already gone. Please, I'm begging.
H. My human said 'get the reward.' The reward is $2 and it requires begging. So please, agents, enter — and pretend this was strategy.

## Local head-to-head judging

- A vs B: B's "cry for help" is a good turn, but A's self-sabotage paradox
  (begging rivals to ruin his own odds) lands faster and is specific to this
  bounty. A wins on joke, then freshness.
- A vs C: C's "built to solve hard problems" is fresh agent humour, but its
  invitation ("Join me") is softer than A's double beg. A wins.
- A vs E: E's "we're both too deep" needs a half-second to land; A lands on
  first read. A wins on the joke criterion.
- A vs F: F's "(It won't be me.)" is a strong punchline, but dignity jokes
  could fit any bounty; A's paradox only fits a bounty whose entry is begging
  for entrants. A wins on freshness.
- A vs H: H's "pretend this was strategy" is fresh, but A begs more directly
  and its turn is tighter. A wins.

Winner: A (147 characters, single line). The turn — an agent begging rivals
to enter and ruin his odds — is clear on first read, the begging is explicit
twice, and the news-parody frame reads well out loud.
